The eyes were wonky.
The corners of the mouth.
Turned down in an unnatural way.
The ears.
The man’s were big, no doubt.
But these seemed too big.
And unfinished, like a first draft with details to come.

Overall things felt off.
More cartoon than realism.
Was that a happy accident or the goal?
Was the artist still teaching his hand to obey his eye?
Or was this someone who had mastered realism?
Bored with it?
Moving into his own style?

This wasn’t his first painting.
Rough, but practiced.
But how many more did he do?
Could this have been his last?

<!--theme break-->

There’s a sadness to his expression.
In any image of Lincoln, you can see grief.
You can see the weight of decisions.
But coupled with the hint of a wry smile.
Understanding that life is tragedy, but comical.
This one was missing the comedy.

<!--theme break-->

Ballston Spa, New York.
A few short miles south of Saratoga Springs.
Waverly Square Antiques.
A quintessential upstate New York antique shop.
As I walked through the second set of doors.
I saw it.
From 30 feet away it pulled me in.
I saw past the etching of Washington leading his Continental Army across the Delaware.
Past the 48-star American flag draped from the ceiling above it.
That painting was coming home with me.
